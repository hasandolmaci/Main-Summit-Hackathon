TASK: Find peer-reviewed or high-quality empirical research (papers, NBER/SSRN working papers, large-sample industry studies) that identifies EARLY, PUBLICLY OBSERVABLE signals with strong measured association to startup success, and that are EASY TO IMPLEMENT from free public web data. Use web search. Do not modify any files (read-only).

CONTEXT: Hackathon project (HTGF track): rank German startups from public traces BEFORE their first institutional round. Success label = first institutional (VC) round within 18 months; also later survival/growth. Our current features are mostly website signals (pricing page, Impressum, customer logos, jobs page, legal entity GmbH/UG, waitlist), grants, GitHub open source, patents, Product Hunt, founder serial experience. Problem found in review: the score partly measures how much data exists; founder signals are nearly empty; Synthesia was missed (stealth site, signal was founders = top CV professors with papers). Papers we already have are listed in ../research-papers.md and metrics in ../scoring-metrics.md (read them; prioritise NEW papers and NEW metrics, but say if an existing one is the strongest evidence).

EASY TO IMPLEMENT means: computable for ~200 German startups in a few hours with free sources, e.g. company website (current + Wayback), Handelsregister/Unternehmensregister/Northdata public snippets, GitHub API, Google Scholar/OpenAlex/Semantic Scholar (free APIs), arXiv, EPO/DPMA open patent data, news/press, LinkedIn via cheap scraping, domain/DNS/crt.sh, app stores. NOT: proprietary survey data, paid PitchBook, internal metrics.

FOR EACH METRIC (aim 10-15, best first) give:
- Metric name and exact operational definition (what to compute, from which free source, point-in-time before as-of date)

- Leakage risk (could the signal be caused by funding instead of predicting it?)

ANSWER FORMAT (max ~900 words): 1) ranked metric table (metric | definition+source | evidence+effect | effort h | stealth-ok | leakage risk); 2) top 5 to implement first and why; 3) which current metrics the literature says are weak or should be down-weighted; 4) list of sources with links; 5) confidence.
